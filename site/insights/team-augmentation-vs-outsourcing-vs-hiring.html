Team augmentation, outsourcing or a new hire: how to choose

Every engineering leader eventually has more roadmap than people. There are three ways to close the gap — and they fail in different ways.

Engagement24 October 20265 min readTechno Software Czech
On this page
  1. Hiring permanently
  2. Outsourcing a project
  3. Team augmentation
  4. Side by side
  5. Choose augmentation when

Hiring permanently

A permanent hire is the right answer for work that is core to your product and will exist for years: the people who own your architecture, your domain logic and your engineering culture.

Its weakness is time. Sourcing, interviews and offers take weeks; in the Czech Republic the statutory notice period is at least two months, so a good candidate who accepts today often starts a quarter from now. And if the role turns out to be temporary, scaling back is slow and expensive.

Outsourcing a project

Outsourcing hands a defined scope to a vendor who delivers it with their own team and process. It works best when the work is separable from your product — a migration, an integration, an internal tool — and can be specified well up front.

It fails when the scope moves. Every change becomes a change request, and when the project ends, the knowledge leaves with the vendor's team.

Team augmentation

With augmentation, engineers join your team. They work in your repositories, attend your stand-ups and report to your leads; the provider recruits, vets, contracts and pays them, and stays accountable for how they perform.

It is the right fit when you already have engineering leadership and a working process, but lack capacity or a specific skill. Its cost is your attention: augmented engineers need onboarding and direction like any team member.

Side by side

HireOutsourceAugment
Who leads the workYouVendorYou
Time to startMonthsWeeksWeeks
Handles changing scopeWellPoorlyWell
Knowledge staysYesMostly noIn your repos and docs
Scaling downHardAt contract endMonth by month

Choose augmentation when

  • your roadmap is clear but your team is too small to deliver it on time;
  • you need a skill for months, not years — AUTOSAR, a payment scheme, a cloud migration;
  • you want to test a role before committing to a permanent headcount;
  • you have leads who can direct the work, and want to keep the code and knowledge in-house.

Most teams end up combining all three: permanent hires for the core, augmentation for capacity and rare skills, and outsourcing for work that can genuinely be handed over.
